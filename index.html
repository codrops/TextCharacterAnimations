Cursor Scramble

After the help panel of SQNCR by halfof8

05:40. The harbour is still grey.

Hana's father takes the boat out early,

as if today were any other day.

Her suitcase waits by the door.

The boat answers her wave with one long note.

STILL GREY

02

Proofread

06:50. At the market, her grandmother

rubs out a price the auctioneer got wrong

and chalks the right one in, without a word.

Hana carries ice from the truck.

The gulls pretend not to watch.

WITHOUT A WORD

03

Telegram

09:30. The tide is out and the boats

lean on the mud, waiting for the water.

Hana walks between them in her father's boots

and reads their names out loud.

One of them still carries her mother's.

THE TIDE IS OUT

04

Loupe

11:00. At the ferry window, her grandmother

writes Hana's ticket out by hand, bent close,

the way she has written every timetable:

tonight, the last crossing, one way.

Hana reads it twice.

ONE WAY

05

Overtype

12:45. Lunch at the noodle counter,

Hana and her grandmother side by side.

The cook puts an extra egg in Hana's bowl

and says it was a mistake.

Nobody believes him. Nobody says so.

ONE EXTRA EGG

06

Autocomplete

18:30. The boats come in with the gulls,

her father's last of all.

He starts to say something about the catch,

and Hana finishes the sentence for him,

the way she always has.

BOATS COME BACK

07

Multi-cursor

19:05. The neighbours come down to the pontoon

and all talk to her at once:

eat properly, write, don't trust the city,

take this, and this, and this.

Her father coils a rope already coiled.

ALL AT ONCE

08

Swipe

19:40. The café windows have fogged up,

and Hana writes her name on the glass

with one finger, in one long stroke,

low down, where the cook will find it

when he wipes the tables in the morning.

20:35. The ticket isn't in the suitcase,

or her coat, or on the counter.

Everyone looks, even the cook.

where she must have kept it all day.

20:45. On the back of a menu, three hands

Voice Note

21:10. Past the breakwater, Hana's phone lights:

a voice note from the end of the pier,

four minutes of wind, a gull, his boots,

and somewhere in the middle, goodnight.

She plays it twice to catch the word.

14

Speed Reader

21:20. On deck she reads the safety card

fast, all the way through, and again,

so she won't look back at the lights.

Round her wrist, a paper streamer

that tore as the ferry pulled away.

DON'T LOOK BACK

15

Screen Reader

21:35. At home, her grandmother asks the tablet

to read the ferry times out loud,

though she wrote every one of them herself.

It reads them one careful word at a time.

She listens all the way to the last.

WORD BY WORD

16

Filament

21:50. The lamps come on along the wall.

The water turns the colour of ink

and holds every light twice.

Her father stays on the pier

until the ferry is just one more of them.

LAMPS ON

17

Photocopy

23:15. The café is closing. On the window,

Hana's name has started to run,

each letter fainter than the one before.

The cook wipes every table

and leaves that pane alone.

LEFT ALONE

18

Photon

00:40. The harbour lights go out one by one.

Her father photographs the sky for her

and gets a picture of nothing but grain.

He sends it anyway.

From somewhere at sea, one comes back.

NOTHING BUT GRAIN

19

Caustics

02:30. The tide turns under the boats,

and the moon breaks on the water in pieces

that keep finding their way back together.

Whatever went out with it tonight

is already on its way back in.
